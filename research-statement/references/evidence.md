# Evidence for the research-statement rules

Compiled 2026-10-28 from 24 sources (`sources.md`). Every quote below was checked by string match against a saved copy of its source. Quotes keep their original spelling; a few are shortened to avoid dashes, never reworded.

## Coverage and gaps

- Five funder or programme sources (NASA Hubble Fellowship 30-year review, DFG Walter Benjamin guidelines 50.10, DFG proposal tips, Schmidt Science Fellows FAQ, Michigan Schmidt AI in Science application page), three physics bodies (AAS postdoc guidelines, Oxford Physics fellowship pages, IOP), three European-fellowship guides (MSCA 2024 national-contact-point handbook, NTNU annotated MSCA template, Imperial MSCA notes), university career offices (Cornell, Harvard FAS, Yale twice, UCLA, Northwestern, Arizona LPL), and individual academics (theoretical physicist Nicole Yunger Halpern, physicist Amanda Peet, mathematician Niven Achenjang, a Trends in Cell Biology career article).
- Mostly US and European. Few sources are specific to high-energy theory postdocs; AAS, Oxford Physics, and Yunger Halpern are the closest.
- Harvard FAS, Cornell, Yale OCS, UCLA, Northwestern, and the Trends in Cell Biology article target faculty statements; they were adapted (independence, balance) rather than copied. The Yale fellowship page targets undergraduates and was used only for general principles.
- The DARPA Heilmeier questions could not be retrieved and are not used. The MIDAS FAQ yielded questions without answers.

## 1. The five questions

| Question | Quotes |
|---|---|
| What and why | Oxford Physics: "How do you propose to tackle this, and what is particularly novel about your approach?" Peet: "Research (what are you doing, and why should they care?)" |
| What you established and your role | Yale OCS: "Even if you worked within a team or with an advisor, clarify where you independently shaped a project’s direction." DFG 50.10: "Indicate clearly throughout the proposal whenever you are referring to your own work or that of other researchers." |
| What next | Yale OCS: "Outline two or three concrete research projects you plan to initiate". Yunger Halpern: "Show that you’ve developed a map and a compass." |
| Why you | Oxford Physics: "Why are you the best person to do this work?" Cornell: "not only that you are knowledgeable, but that you are the person to carry out the research." |
| Why here, why now | Oxford Physics: "Why at Oxford and why now?" Yale fellowship: "why is it important that you conduct your project there instead of elsewhere?" |

## 2. Rules and their sources

### First page
- Harvard FAS: "Search committee members are extraordinarily busy, so you must structure your statement strategically". NTNU: "get them to buy into your project proposal from the first page."
- Yale OCS: "Start your research statement by briefly introducing the central questions that drive your inquiry."
- Trends in Cell Biology: "It is not a personal essay".

### One programme
- Yunger Halpern: "Cast the key projects in the form of a story" and "What vision underlies the program?"; "Not all past projects deserve the same amount of space. Tell a cohesive story."
- Cornell: "You probably want to select an over-arching theme for your research statement and leave some miscellaneous ideas or projects out of it."
- Schmidt Science Fellows: "You should focus on creating a single clear Fellowship Research Proposal."

### Past work as evidence
- AAS: "Summaries of two to three completed/published studies that are most relevant to advertised position".
- Yunger Halpern: "Avoid verbs that fail to relate that you accomplished anything: “studied,” “investigated,” “worked on,” etc." and "Which technical or conceptual contribution you provided"; "name-dropping venues instead can convey arrogance."
- IOP: "Where you refer to collaborative activities you should ensure your personal role is clear."
- Achenjang: "try to finish them (or, at least, get a version on arxiv) before applying".

### Future plan
- AAS: "If position is an institutional Fellowship, balance should be ~⅓ prior/current work and ~⅔ proposed research". Harvard FAS: future directions "about 50-60% of the overall 3-5 page statement". UCLA gives a 25% past, 25% current, 50% future example.
- NTNU: "Two to three research objectives are appropriate"; "projects based on explanatory and/or hypothesis-driven approaches (or with clearly defined research questions) do much better than those based on exploratory or descriptive".
- Harvard FAS: long, medium, and "What you’ll tackle from the get-go(low-hanging fruits)". Peet: "Short- & medium- term plans, long-term goals."
- DFG tips: "The hypotheses or (depending on the subject) objectives of your project should follow on logically from the state of the art in research."

### Feasibility
- DFG tips: "Include alternative strategies and explain how you will deal with any possible risks."; "very high-risk approaches should be combined with less risk-based work packages"; "Demonstrate that you are able to successfully implement the planned project by presenting your own preliminary work."
- NTNU: place riskier parts towards the end of the work plan so the project does not collapse early.
- NASA Hubble review: "Feasibility of proposed project within the fellowship timescale".
- Cornell: "Research statements can be weakened by overly ambitious proposals, by lack of clear direction". Yale OCS: "Proposing projects that are unrealistic or lack clear objectives can hurt your credibility."

### Independence
- Achenjang: "You should not only continue your PhD work, but should instead show people you have the ability to do your own mathematics beyond just following your advisor’s".
- Northwestern: "you are not talking as a graduate student. You are not proposing a dissertation or potential experiment."
- Oxford Physics: "fellows expected to develop their own independent research programme".
- Arizona LPL: "the postdoc research area cannot be so different that you are too clueless."

### Fit
- Yale OCS: "Generic statements that don’t align with the department’s strengths, facilities, or priorities will fail to convince committees".
- Imperial: "it’s not sufficient to state that this exists, you need to relate it to the project you have described."
- Oxford Physics: "Get in contact with potential collaborators in the Department of Physics to discuss your ideas". Achenjang: "it is better to ask them earlier rather than later".

### Outsider readers
- Cornell: "It should be technical, but should remain intelligible to any member of the department."
- MSCA handbook: "The majority of evaluators will not be expert in the specific subject area".
- Yunger Halpern: "don’t describe only systems, subsystems, and control; invoke atoms, cavities, and lasers."
- Harvard FAS: "Use subtitles". AAS: "Intermingle a few self-explanatory figures with short captions".
- DFG 50.10: "The description must be understandable without referring to additional literature."

### Interdisciplinary and AI
- Oxford Schmidt AI: "Core AI research is not supported."; "Clear grasp of how AI will be used and its potential benefits"; "assume reviewers may not have expertise in your specific scientific domain or AI methods."
- MIDAS: "evidence of excellence and a passion for the proposed research direction at the intersection of two disciplines."; "the effect of flawed data, brittle analyses, and irreproducible results."
- NTNU: "evaluators have a marked preference for inter-disciplinary projects". DFG: "Interdisciplinary proposals are always welcome."

### Honesty and citation
- Yale fellowship: "Do not exaggerate your accomplishments, but also do not be falsely modest."
- DFG tips: "reviewers notice right away if literature references are missing."
- NTNU: "an inadequate description of the state-of-the-art is a common weakness of proposals".

### Format
- MSCA handbook: evaluators mark by checklist, and misplaced information means "they will give you “zero” for that sub-criterion"; write in the singular "as this is your individual fellowship, not the application of a research group."
- UCLA: "Try not to have too much language repetition across documents".
- Yunger Halpern: "End on a strong note, presenting a coherent vision."
- Cornell: "There are no excuses for spelling errors."

### The person
- NASA Hubble review: "the best scientist is selected, not (just) the science". DFG 50.10 assesses "potential and career prospects" as well as qualifications.

## 3. Conflicts between sources

| Issue | Side A | Side B | Decision in SKILL.md |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verbs | Harvard FAS: replace "explore" with "examine" or "investigate" | Yunger Halpern, NTNU: "investigate, study, explore" state no outcome | Outcome verbs for past work, product verbs for objectives |
| Ambition | Harvard FAS: "Go big!"; Cornell: say so if the work could answer big questions | Cornell, Yale OCS: over-ambition weakens a statement | Big question, bounded deliverables |
| Balance | AAS fellowships ⅔ future; Harvard 50~60% | AAS general postings: about one page each of completed, ongoing, proposed | 1:2 fellowships, 1:1 PI-group postdocs |
| Distance from PhD | Schmidt Science Fellows: a genuine change of focus; Arizona LPL: a topic different from the dissertation | Arizona LPL: not so different that competence disappears | Big pivot only where the scheme demands it |
