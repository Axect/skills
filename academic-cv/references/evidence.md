# Evidence for the academic-cv rules

Compiled 2026-10-28 from 22 sources (`sources.md`). Every quote below was string-matched against a saved copy of its source.

## Coverage and gaps

- University career offices: Berkeley (CV elements), MIT CAPD, Penn, Toronto, Cornell postdoc office, Michigan, UC Davis, Texas A&M, Oxford, Cambridge, Sydney. Funders: DFG (CV FAQ, publication-list FAQ), ERC (2024 evaluation changes, narrative CV article), Humboldt Research Fellowship, NASA Hubble Fellowship 30-year review. Physics and astronomy: AAS postdoc application guidelines, AAS Journals author contribution statements, Max Planck Institute for Solar System Research (CV guide), Amanda Peet (Toronto physics), Niven Achenjang (mathematics).
- Mostly faculty-oriented US guidance; AAS, Peet, and MPS are the physics-specific voices. The UW career page could not be parsed.

## Purpose

- Toronto: "Because the CV is read alongside the cover letter, research statement, and teaching materials, its role is to document rather than interpret."
- Peet: "Dossier: Cover letter (hello), CV (past), research plan (future)."
- Penn: "The purpose of the CV is to interest a committee in interviewing you."
- Berkeley: "what are the three things you want them to know about you as they near the end of the first page".

## Order

- MIT: "Material you present early in your CV is likely to stand out more than material placed later. (An exception is that publications are often at the end)." and "If applying for a position that primarily involves research, describe research experience first; if the reverse is true, put teaching experience first."
- Berkeley: "Aside from the first two (contact information and education/postdoc(s)) which generally lead, the order that follows depends on your own particular strengths and the nature of the job you are applying".
- Penn: "Most PhD candidates will want to have the education section listed as their first category, while postdocs might want to have “Current Research Experience” as a first category."
- Oxford: "For experienced research staff, this may be above the "Education" section."
- UC Davis: "Hiring and funding committees are looking for well-organized documents with the most relevant content presented first."
- MPS: "Start with the most important information (got an important prize? Make sure it’s on the first page!)"

## Chronology and dates

- Cornell: "List items within each category chronologically, the most recent appearing first."
- Texas A&M: "In every section put information in reverse chronology."
- Sydney: "You should provide dates for everything."

## Length

- Toronto: early-career CVs typically run two to four pages, and "Clarity and organization matter more than brevity."
- MIT: "Completeness is more important than brevity."
- AAS: "Not to exceed two pages, excluding list of publications".
- DFG: "Yes, the CV must not exceed four pages". ERC: "the Curriculum Vitae and Track Record will be merged into one document of up to four pages". Humboldt: "curriculum vitae (two pages max)".
- Peet: "Important to leave some things out!"

## Tailoring and a master CV

- MIT: "You should have two CVs: an authoritative CV with all information, and a tailored CV, written for a specific position or opportunity."
- Berkeley: "Consequently, you should expect to have two to three versions of your CV by the end of the application process."
- Oxford: "Sometimes organisations will give guidance (on their website/recruitment materials) on what they want you to include in your CV - if they do, follow it."
- MPS: "Keep a full version of your CV including everything potentially relevant on your Overleaf /".

## Publications and status

- AAS: "Separate under "refereed", "conference proceedings/abstracts", and "software" categories".
- Texas A&M: "List all in reverse chronological order, but subdivide by Published/Accepted; Under Review; In Progress" and "For under review, indicate name of journal or conference where under submission".
- Berkeley, on listing work as in press: "You should only do this if your work has in fact been accepted, and not if it has only been submitted or is under review."
- Sydney: "make this status very clear (e.g. under review, accepted for publication, in press)".
- MPS: "if you want to include unpublished papers, indicate their status (submitted or in prep.)" and "make a clear distinction between refereed journal papers and conference proceedings".
- Penn: "You can list a few articles that are in preparation and will realistically be published."
- DFG: "You are not allowed to list manuscripts with the following statuses: in preparation, submitted (to), in review, in revision or similar." and "you are not permitted to state the journal submission status of manuscripts that have already been published as preprints."
- Achenjang: "try to finish them (or, at least, get a version on arxiv) before applying to postdocs, so you can include them in your cv".

## Own role

- AAS: "For multi-author refereed papers, include one-line summary of applicant's contributions".
- AAS Journals: "members of the community often attempt to infer something about author contributions from the ordering of the author list, such as when reviewing applications for jobs, promotion and tenure packets" and "as names must be listed sequentially in some way, co-leadership of a paper cannot easily be indicated."
- Berkeley: "One way to ensure that both are conspicuous is to bold your **name** in the author list". MIT: "Put your name in bold to highlight your authorship". UC Davis: "Publications: Full citations with your name bolded".
- ERC: "A short explanation of the significance of the selected outputs, the applicant’s role in producing each of them".
- Humboldt assesses "evaluation of your own contributions to publications involving several authors".
- MIT: "When describing your experience, include the goal, your contributions, and the impact/result of your contributions."
- Sydney: "It’s imperative to state your role/position on a grant".

## Recognition

- Berkeley: "Don’t overestimate the recognizability of your awards and honors beyond your immediate circle."
- Penn: "Foreign students, in particular, should stress the degree to which an unfamiliar award was competitive."
- Sydney: "So, explain that it’s competitive. Better still, state the success rate, if you know it." and "you don’t want to bury a fellowship halfway down a list of grants where someone skim-reading your CV might miss it."
- Texas A&M: "List all received or pending, but not rejections".

## Talks

- AAS: "Limit to about five highest-visibility talks". MPS: "highlight invited talks".
- Sydney: "the *type* of presentation (e.g. keynote, plenary, poster, seminar)".
- MIT: "Conference presentations should include: Title, Name of conference or event, Location, Date".

## Metrics

- ERC: "the use of Journal Impact Factors is not allowed in our evaluations".
- DFG: research outcomes "are to be made more visible than before, with a shift away from metrics."
- Sydney: "The inconsistent use of metrics looks either careless or suspect, like you’re trying to hide something."
- Toronto: such reforms "have had limited impact on job application CVs, which generally continue to follow traditional formats."

## Format

- MIT: "Use a consistent format throughout, use an easy to read font, and make good use of descriptive section headings, subheadings, and white space to guide your reader."
- Toronto: "Use a single, readable font (minimum 11‑point size)." Cambridge: "Don’t go below 10pt."
- Berkeley: "You should also put your name and the page number in a header or footer after the first page."
- Oxford: "Use simple language" and avoid "jargon".
- DFG: "Please remove all grey and red text after filling in the CV and do not attach a photograph. The template formatting must be retained."

## Funder templates

- DFG: "you can list up to 10 publications in a publication medium of category A (journals with peer review, conferences with peer review or anthology volumes, and books)" and "Examples of elements of category B include preprints, clinical trial protocols, data sets, patents etc."
- ERC: "a list of up to ten research outputs that demonstrate how they have advanced knowledge in their field, with an emphasis on more recent achievements".
- Humboldt: applications that "diverge substantially from our specifications e.g. for creating a list of publications, will be formally rejected without" review.

## Referees

- AAS: "One page with contact information for three to five people from whom letters of reference may be requested".
- Oxford: "For academic CVs, it is typical to list referees".
- Penn: "While it is really up to you as to whether you list your references on the CV, a majority of job candidates do just that."
