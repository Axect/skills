# Evidence for the research-proposal rules

Compiled 2026-10-28 from 17 sources (`sources.md`). Every quote below was string-matched against a saved copy of its source; quotes containing dashes in the original are shortened, never reworded.

## Coverage and gaps

- Funders: ERC (How to write your proposal, 2025; changes to the 2026 and 2027 work programmes; Starting Grant page), DFG (Walter Benjamin guidelines 50.10 and proposal tips), Humboldt Foundation (Research Fellowship page and information for hosts), NSF (preparing a proposal; PAPPG chapter II), Schmidt programmes (Michigan AI in Science application, Schmidt Science Fellows FAQ), Oxford Physics (fellowship applications, Schmidt AI internal selection). ## The funder's questions

- NSF: the project description "details what the proposer wants to do, why they want to do it, how they plan to do it, how they will know if they succeed, and what benefits could accrue if the project is successful".
- ERC (Part I): it "should describe the overall idea of the proposed research project" and "the scientific questions the project will attempt to answer, the objectives of the project, and the overall approach or research strategy to achieve those objectives".
- ERC (two steps): "“Is this a great idea that would be worth pursuing?”" then "“Can that idea be pursued realistically, and if so, in the manner and with the approach that the applicant proposes?”"
- Oxford Physics: "What is the scientific problem or question that you propose to address?"; "How do you propose to tackle this, and what is particularly novel about your approach?"; "What would be the step change for the field/world if this work was funded?"
- Humboldt (to hosts): "What questions are to be examined? What is the significance of these questions for the advancement of the research area?"

## A sharp question and ambition

- ERC: "First and foremost, applicants will be assessed on the ambition of their idea in advancing present-day knowledge in the field based on Part I of the Scientific Proposal." and "Think big, beyond the next paper".
- Ten Simple Rules: "“Me-too” science will not get funded when funding levels are low. On the other hand, science that is too speculative will not be supported either".
- NTNU: projects "with clearly defined research questions" do better than "exploratory or descriptive approaches (“fishing expeditions”)".
- Humboldt (to hosts): "a further criterion that plays a crucial role in the independent peer reviewers’ and Selection Committee’s evaluation of an application is the originality of the research proposal".

## Objectives

- DFG tips: "The hypotheses or (depending on the subject) objectives of your project should follow on logically from the state of the art in research."
- NTNU: "A commonly recurring weakness picked up by the evaluators is that the project’s objectives are too broad, i.e., that they are not specific enough." and "Refrain from using “weak verbs” like “investigate, study, explore, learn, understand” as they are rather broad and do not suggest a concrete outcome."
- Imperial: "Give a short general introduction then explain immediately what you are planning to do, what your aims are, and what the benefits of the project will be."

## State of the art

- DFG 50.10: "Explain briefly and precisely the state of the art in your field in its direct relationship to your project."
- NTNU: "In our experience, an inadequate description of the state-of-the-art is a common weakness of proposals (it is either missing entirely or too general)." and "Explain, in concrete terms, how your proposed research will overcome these limitations and go beyond the state-of-the-art (i.e. how is it conceptually novel?)."
- Humboldt (to hosts): "What is the current state of research (evidenced by approx. five relevant publications from the research area; one page max)?"
- DFG tips: "Incidentally, reviewers notice right away if literature references are missing. Be sure to avoid this error!" Ten Simple Rules: "make sure to cite all relevant work".

## Methods

- DFG 50.10: "What methods are already available? What methods need to be developed?"
- Imperial: "Describe your method in enough detail that another researcher would understand how you intend to conduct the work, including why you’ve chose these approaches compared to any alternatives."
- DFG tips: "good projects always take into account the entire spectrum of possible contemporary methods" and not "only those in which you are well versed."
- NTNU: "A common weakness in lower scoring proposals is that the WPs and methodology are not presented in sufficient detail."

## Work plan

- NTNU: "In MSCA-PF projects we recommend applying a 1:1 ratio between research objectives and WPs" and "Importantly, the arrows between the WPs must indicate identifiable flows of results (data/knowledge) within the project."
- Imperial: "establish measurable Milestones and verifiable Deliverables and describe them clearly enough that your progress through the project could be assessed against them."
- MSCA handbook: "The achievement of a milestone should be verifiable."
- Ten Simple Rules: "A common temptation for inexperienced grant writers is to propose to do too much. Such applications are usually judged as overly ambitious and consequently poorly rated."
- Humboldt: reviewers look for "clear focus and feasibility within the requested sponsorship period".

## Risks

- DFG tips: "Include alternative strategies and explain how you will deal with any possible risks." and "Ideally, very high-risk approaches should be combined with less risk-based work packages within a project."
- NTNU: "Preferably, the riskier parts should be located towards the end of your work plan, such that the entire project will not fall apart in the early stages if a risk cannot be mitigated."
- MSCA handbook: "A critical risk is a plausible event or issue that could have a high adverse impact on the ability of the project to achieve its objectives."
- ERC: "Which are the main risks and how I can mitigate them?"

## Feasibility and preliminary results

- DFG tips: "Demonstrate that you are able to successfully implement the planned project by presenting your own preliminary work."
- Ten Simple Rules: "do provide some tantalizing preliminary result, making sure to tell the reviewers what these results imply with respect to the specific aims of your proposal."
- Imperial: "Show that what you’re proposing is possible within the time you have."
- ERC (2026 onward): "Part I of the Scientific Proposal should no longer include details on the feasibility of the scientific approach but present a convincing overall research strategy."
- DFG tips: "Results that have already been obtained" and published "cannot be considered as the basis for a new project idea."
- DFG 50.10: "Indicate clearly throughout the proposal whenever you are referring to your own work or that of other researchers."

## Host and environment

- Oxford Physics: "Why at Oxford and why now?"
- Imperial: "it’s not sufficient to state that this exists, you need to relate it to the project you have described."
- DFG 50.10: "In particular, describe the support you will receive there for your project (financial resources, work opportunities, resources) as well as for the further development of your career."
- Humboldt: the host "will provide you with a mentoring agreement and written confirmation that research facilities are available, and will compile a detailed review for you."
- Humboldt (to hosts): "First, applicants must coordinate their independently developed research proposal with you."
- MIDAS: "Whether the selection of mentors is appropriate for the research project, the training plan, and career development, as well as consistency among the mentor’s letters".

## Resources

- DFG tips: "the scope of the funds applied for must derive consistently from the work programme."
- ERC: "only unjustified requests can be cut, so do not artificially inflate your budget".
- Ten Simple Rules: "Specify the scope up-front and make sure it is realistic with respect to the funds requested."
- Oxford Physics: "make sure you understand what can be accomplished with the funding on offer so that your proposal is realistic".

## Audience and clarity

- DFG tips: "Reviewers are often quite busy" and "the more concise and clear the presentation of your project, the easier it will be to assess."
- Ten Simple Rules: "It is a skill to capture the interest of experts and nonexperts alike. Develop that skill." and "Each section can start with a summary of the key points."
- DFG 50.10: "The description must be understandable without referring to additional literature." and "Note that reviewers are not required to read any of the works you cite."
- Imperial: "Don’t rely on web links to show any additional material to evaluators: they are instructed to disregard anything which isn’t part of the body text."
- NTNU: "The evaluators are busy people; therefore, get them to buy into your project proposal from the first page."

## Compliance

- MSCA handbook: "Evaluators use a “checklist” approach to marking" and misplaced information means "they will give you “zero” for that sub-criterion."; "All tables, figures, references and any other element pertaining to these sections must be included as an integral part of these sections and they are counted towards this page limit."
- NSF PAPPG: "NSF will not accept or will return without review proposals that are not consistent with these instructions." and "PIs are advised that readability is of paramount importance and should take precedence in selection of an appropriate font for use in the proposal."
- Humboldt: "research outline ( five pages max)" and applications that "diverge substantially from our specifications" are rejected.
- Imperial: "Have the Work Programme, Guide for Applicants and the evaluation criteria open as you work through the proposal".
- Ten Simple Rules: "Many funding bodies will immediately triage grants that do not comply with the guidelines" and "Your goal should be to provide a complete reckoning of what is to be done, as briefly as possible."

## Summary and impact

- MSCA handbook (abstract): "It should communicate the importance, impact and timeliness of the project and also convince the evaluator that you should be funded to carry it out." and "The abstract and keywords are used to select the evaluators."
- NSF: the one-page project summary "provides an overview of the proposed activity, a statement on its intellectual merit, and a statement on its" broader impacts.
- Imperial: "Be specific: how many articles do you expect to publish? Which journals will you aim to publish in? Which conferences do you want to present your work at?"
- MIDAS: "The statement should also describe the feasibility of the initial scope of the proposed research and its potential to grow into a sustained research program for an independent research career."

## Data and integrity

- DFG 50.10: "Should your project not use or generate data to a relevant extent, please explicitly state this to be the case."
- MIDAS: "all researchers need to take into consideration the effect of flawed data, brittle analyses, and irreproducible results."
- Imperial: "Remember that Open Access principles are expected to be a part of your project."
